# Brick Racers — Fancy Car Showroom — Design Spec

Date: 2026-10-17
Builds on: the base game, mini city (3a/3b), supercars + offline, and play-together specs.

## Purpose
- **Murad (8, iPad) keeps asking about Rolls-Royce.** It's a different kind of fancy from the supercars he already has (Lamborghini, Ferrari, Bugatti, McLaren): posh, full of clever little tricks, not just fast.
- **His parent wants more surprises** in the game for Murad and his friends: things he discovers rather than things he's told about.
- So: a posh showroom in the city keeps a **mystery car under a silk sheet**. Collecting coins uncovers it, and the car arrives **ready-made** in his garage as a prize. Then the next mystery car rolls in, again and again.

## Decisions (from the user)
- Fancy cars, Rolls-Royce first.
- Prize cars arrive **ready-made**, unlocked as a prize (he doesn't build them step by step).
- The showroom **keeps revealing a new fancy car every so often**, not just one.
- The lineup, the tricks, the coin goals and the batches below were proposed by the designer and approved by the user ("write it up").

## Goals
- A reveal moment that feels like a gift: drumroll, the sheet whooshes off, confetti, the car turning in the light.
- Each car has **one or two real-life tricks** he finds by playing with it (the Rolls-Royce's door umbrella, its starry roof).
- There is always a next mystery to chase, so the coins he collects finally mean something.
- Friends in Play together see his prize cars and can wonder together what's under the sheet.

## Non-goals
- Not a shop. Nothing has a price, nothing is "bought", and coins are never spent.
- Not in this batch: the red-carpet hotel, the golden car in traffic, and the gold paint shop. These are separate ideas for later.
- Prize cars are not editable brick projects (see "Prize cars in My cars").

## Anti-goals (it would be wrong if…)
- **Coins go down.** The saved pile only ever goes up (`js/progress.js` already promises this). Unlocking never takes coins away.
- **The next car is spoiled.** No on-screen text, card or voice says what's under the sheet before the reveal. Only the sheet's shape hints at it.
- **A prize car is lost:** deleted by an accidental tap, wiped by an edit, or left without a place to live.
- **A prize car becomes the fastest.** The Bugatti stays the speed king. Fancy cars feel special, not faster.
- **Murad's name is announced.** "Murad Motors" appears quietly on the showroom sign, like the pizza shop and stunt park signs. Nothing points it out.
- **It feels like grinding.** The first goal must be reachable in a play session or two.

## Constraints
- Same stack: Three.js 0.170 + cannon-es 0.20, ES modules, no build step, `node:test`. Pure rule modules stay free of three and the DOM.
- **Works offline.** Everything is local. The offline file list is generated by `server.js`, so new files need nothing extra.
- Kid-safe and touch-first; a non-reader can follow it (🔊 read-outs, pictures over words).
- **Name clash:** `js/showroom.js` already exists (the home/garage background with the spinning car). The new city building needs a different module name.
- **Brand names** are real for this private family game. Badges are drawn the same way as the supercar badges (`js/badgeArt.js` + `js/brandLogos.js`: the real logo, or a look-alike until it loads). The README note about swapping brand names before any public release covers these too.
- **Play together:** cars are sent as brick lists (`js/net/together.js`), and the server checks every brick with `isValidBrick` (`server/rooms.js`). New brick types must be valid on the server (same deploy). A tablet that meets a brick type it doesn't know (an older version, before it updates) must skip it rather than break.

## The showroom
- **Where:** the "SHOP · coming soon" building site on block (3, 2) (`shopSoon` in `js/cityMap.js`, drawn in `js/cityWorld.js` and `js/cityProps.js`). The site becomes the showroom, and the scaffolding and the cones go away.
- **Look:** a posh building with a big glass front facing the road, a sign that reads **MURAD MOTORS** (matching the existing `hero` brand name), and inside, a round turntable slowly spinning with a car under a silk sheet with a big "?" on it.
- **The sheet's shape** follows the car underneath (tall and long for the Rolls-Royce, tall and boxy for the G-Wagon, very long and low for the limo). This is the only hint.
- **The goal sign:** walking or driving up to the front shows a big sign with a progress bar: `🪙 37 / 150` and the "?" sheet. 🔊 reads "What's under the sheet? Collect 150 coins to find out!". Words like "buy", "price" or "cost" are never used.
- **On the mini-map** the showroom shows as a "?" spot. When a goal is reached, it gently sparkles so he knows to come back. (The exact look is the builder's call.)

## Earning a car
- Each mystery has a **coin goal**, counted from the moment that mystery appeared. The game remembers his coin pile at that moment and compares it with his pile now. Every coin added to the pile counts, wherever it came from.
- **Goals (my call):** 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 450, one per car in lineup order. The city has about 126 coins' worth on the map, coming back every 90 seconds, so batch 1 (600 coins) may be finished within days. That's acceptable: the first reveals should come quickly, and the "coming soon" display (below) keeps counting toward the next batch.
- **The first mystery starts** the first time he opens the city after this update. Coins collected earlier don't count, so the first reveal is earned, but it's still reachable in a session or two.
- When the pile reaches the goal, the coin counter could give a little extra cheer. The reveal itself **waits for him to come to the showroom**, so it always happens in front of the turntable.

## The reveal
1. He arrives at the showroom with the goal reached (on foot or in a car; if driving, the camera takes over gently).
2. Drumroll → the sheet whooshes off → confetti and a fanfare → the car turns slowly in a spotlight while 🔊 says its name ("A Rolls-Royce Phantom!").
3. The car **drives itself out to his garage** and parks in a free numbered spot with a gold ribbon on its roof. The ribbon goes away the first time he drives it. Exactly how it gets there (driving the streets, a quick fade, or a short flight of the camera) is the builder's call. It must be clear that the car is now his and where it went.
4. The next sheet-covered car rolls onto the turntable, with a new goal.
- **If the garage is full** (all 24 spots taken), the prize car still goes into My cars and can always be raced. In the city it waits on the showroom forecourt until a spot frees up. It is never lost.
- **When the built lineup runs out** (after the limo in batch 1), the turntable shows the cars he has unlocked taking turns, with a small "More cars coming soon ✨" sign and no "?" sheet and no goal (my call). The save still remembers his coin pile at the moment of the last reveal. When a later batch adds the next car, its goal counts from that moment, so if he kept collecting, the next mystery may already be earned and is revealed on his next visit. Batches 2 and 3 must not need any change to old saves.
- **After the very last car** (the golden Rolls-Royce), the same collection display, without the "coming soon" sign.
- **In Play together:** the reveal happens on his own tablet only. Each friend has their own showroom progress on their own tablet; nothing is shared or unlocked for everyone, and two friends may see different sheets on the same building. That's fine.
- **Friends see his prize car's look**, sent as bricks like any car: the grille, the flying lady, the badge, the long limo body. The moving tricks (statue rising, doors swinging, umbrella, starry-roof camera, disco lights, side-pipe puff) play **on his own tablet only** (my call). No new play-together message types are needed for this batch. The existing confetti and honk syncing stays as it is.
- If he reaches the goal while in a race or a room game, nothing interrupts. The reveal waits for his next visit to the showroom.

## Prize cars in My cars
- Each prize car appears in **🚗 My cars** with a 🏆 mark. He can **🏁 Race** it and drive it in the city like any car.
- **No Change, no Delete** on prize cars (my call), so a tap can't lose one and an edit can't wreck its tricks.
- Prize cars are **ready-made brick cars** in the same toy-brick look as everything else, made from a fixed recipe like the supercar models (`js/instructions.js`). They include new pieces made for them (below).
- **Prize-only pieces and badges (my call):** the new pieces (grille, flying lady, coach door, starlight roof, off-road wheel, spare wheel, long chassis, disco lights…) and the new brand badges are **not** in the builder's tray or the "Which badge?" picker. Otherwise they'd spoil the next mysteries. This goes against today's rule that every part except the chassis is in exactly one tray tab (`js/bricks.js`), so that rule and its test change. Opening the prize pieces up for his own builds could be a later surprise; it's not in this batch.
- **Tricks belong to the pieces:** a car with a flying-lady piece gets the rising statue, a coach door gets the backwards opening and the umbrella, and so on. Since the pieces only come in prize cars, that's the same as tricks belonging to the prize cars, and no separate "trick" mark has to be saved.
- **The prize mark** (for the 🏆 and for hiding Change/Delete) has to survive `storage.save()`, which today keeps only known fields, so saving learns to keep it. The play-together car clean-up on the server doesn't need to keep it; friends don't need to know it's a prize.
- **Speeds:** all inside the existing `LIMITS` (`js/carStats.js`), and none as fast as the Bugatti Chiron. *My guess:* the Rolls-Royce is smooth and quiet with a softer engine sound, the G-Wagon pulls strongly up slopes, and the limo is slow and stately.
- Saved alongside his other cars (`js/storage.js`), marked as prizes, and they count toward the garage spots like any car. Which cars he has unlocked is also remembered separately, so a prize car can be brought back if its saved copy ever goes missing.

## The lineup
Built in batches. **Batch 1 is what gets built now.** Batches 2 and 3 are recorded here so the showroom order is known, and get their own spec later.

### Batch 1 (build now)
**1. Rolls-Royce Phantom** (goal 150). Long, tall, black and silver. The brand badge is the double R.
- **Flying lady:** a little silver statue on the bonnet rises when he gets in and sinks out of sight when he gets out.
- **Coach doors:** the doors open backwards (hinged at the back) when he gets in or out.
- **Door umbrella:** when he gets out, an umbrella pops out of the door and his figure holds it for a few seconds before putting it back. (*My guess:* every time, because it's cheap and funny. The builder may make it every few times if it gets in the way.)
- **Starlight roof:** the inside of the roof twinkles with little stars. The first time he gets in, the camera tilts up to show it.
- **New pieces:** tall chrome grille, flying-lady ornament, coach door, starlight roof panel, and the Rolls-Royce badge.

**2. Mercedes-AMG G-Wagon** (goal 200). Boxy, tall, with chunky off-road tyres and a spare wheel on the back.
- **Climber:** it powers up the stunt-park ramps and garage ramps without slowing down. (A strong pull on slopes, still within `LIMITS`.)
- **Side pipes** that puff when it starts.
- **New pieces:** boxy body panels, spare wheel, chunky off-road wheel, side exhaust, and the Mercedes star badge. (`star` is already the `hero` emblem's name, so the Mercedes emblem needs its own.)

**3. Stretch Limousine** (goal 250). White, ridiculously long, with a Murad Motors badge (my call: the limo has no real brand, so it's the showroom's own).
- **Disco lights:** coloured lights inside flash in time when he honks.
- **Really long:** about twice as long as a normal car. The usual chassis is a fixed 4 × 8 studs with the wheel slots at fixed corners (`js/carStats.js`, `js/instructions.js`), so the limo needs a longer chassis, or another way to make it long that still drives well. How is the builder's call. It must look clearly, comically long and still be fun to steer.
- **Bendy corners (nice to have):** it looks like it bends a little round corners. The builder may drop this if the physics fights it.
- **New pieces:** long chassis (or equivalent), limo window strip, disco light panel.

### Batch 2 (later)
4. **Bentley Continental GT** (goal 300): the dashboard spins round to show a different face, like the real rotating display.
5. **Rolls-Royce Cullinan** (goal 350): the boot opens and two little seats fold out to sit and watch the view.

### Batch 3 (later)
6. **Koenigsegg** (goal 400): doors that swing out and twist upward (dihedral doors), the fanciest doors of all.
7. **Golden Rolls-Royce** (goal 450): the finale, all of it shiny gold.

## Saving
- Showroom progress is saved on the tablet like coins and missions: which mystery is on the turntable, the coin pile when it started, and which cars are unlocked. It sits next to (or inside) `brickRacers.progress.v1`, and old saves without it load as "first mystery, not started yet".
- It is checked when loaded (like `progress.js` and `storage.js` do), so a broken save never breaks the city.

## Testing
- Pure rules (goal reached, next mystery, coins never spent, save/load with bad data, the full-garage case, prize cars can't be deleted) get `node:test` tests like the existing modules.
- Every prize car recipe passes `raceReadiness` and stays inside `LIMITS`, and none is faster than the Bugatti. A test checks this.
- New brick types pass `isValidBrick` on the server, and a prize car can be sent in a play-together room. A test checks this.
- A way to try the reveal without collecting 150 coins (a console debug hook like the existing play-together ones) so a grown-up can check it on the iPad.

## Left to the builder
- Module names and file layout (avoiding the `js/showroom.js` clash), and how the showroom building is drawn.
- Exact animation timings, sounds and camera moves for the reveal and the tricks.
- How the prize car gets from the showroom to the garage.
- How the limo is made long, and whether it bends.
- The mini-map look for the showroom.
- Exact piece sizes and colours, within the toy-brick look.
